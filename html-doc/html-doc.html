The Life of a Shark

In the depths of the ocean lives a shark. Her day doesn’t start with the sunrise — light doesn’t matter much underwater. She swims slowly and confidently, sensing everything around her: tiny movements in the water, smells, even the electrical signals of other creatures. The shark is never in a hurry. She can travel hundreds of kilometers without getting tired. She has no bones — only cartilage, making her body flexible and light. Her skin is covered with tiny scales that feel like teeth, allowing her to glide through the water with almost no resistance. The shark hunts. Sometimes it’s a fast chase, sometimes a long wait. She doesn’t eat every hour. She can go days, even weeks, without food. But when prey is near — she strikes with perfect precision. The shark is not evil. She is simply alive. In her world, there is no good or bad — only nature, the rules of survival, and a delicate balance. And so the shark lives — an ancient, powerful, and nearly perfect creature, one that has ruled the oceans for hundreds of millions of years.\
